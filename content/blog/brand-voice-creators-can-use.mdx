Most brand voice documents are written for people who already know the brand.
They say things like "confident but approachable" and "expert without being
intimidating," and they are completely useless to a freelance scriptwriter on
their first day.

A voice guide earns its existence when someone outside the building can write a
passable first draft from it.

## Replace adjectives with pairs

"Confident" means nothing on its own. "Confident, not certain" means something —
it tells a writer to make claims but leave room for nuance. Every trait gets a
counterweight:

- Warm, not chummy.
- Direct, not blunt.
- Expert, not academic.
- Playful, not quirky.

The second half does the real work. It marks the edge the writer shouldn't cross.

## Show the rewrite, not the rule

For each trait, include a line written badly and the same line written well.
Three pairs beat three paragraphs of description. People copy patterns far more
reliably than they follow instructions.

## Fix the small decisions

Half of what makes writing sound off-brand is mechanical, and all of it is
easy to specify once: contractions or not, em dashes or not, how you refer to
the customer, whether you use the product name as a verb, sentence case or title
case in headings, emoji policy by platform, and the handful of words you never
use.

Writers make these decisions dozens of times per script. Deciding them centrally
removes an enormous amount of inconsistency for very little effort.

## Write down what you don't sound like

Naming two or three brands you're adjacent to but deliberately unlike is often
the fastest way to communicate a voice. "Like X, but without the hard sell" lands
in one line what a page of adjectives can't.

## Test it on a stranger

The only real validation: hand the guide to someone who has never worked on the
brand, ask for a short script, and see what comes back. Whatever they get wrong
is what the document is missing. Fix that, and nothing else, then test again.

A voice guide isn't finished when it's comprehensive. It's finished when someone
new can use it.
