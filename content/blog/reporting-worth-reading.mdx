There's a version of social reporting that exists to prove work happened. It has
every metric the platform exposes, a lot of green arrows, and no recommendation
anywhere in it. Nobody reads it twice.

The alternative is shorter and considerably more uncomfortable to write.

## Ask what would change your mind

Before picking metrics, write down the decisions the report is meant to inform.
Usually there are only a few: what to make more of, what to stop making, where
to put paid support, and whether the overall direction is working.

Then keep only the numbers that could actually move one of those decisions. A
metric that goes up or down without changing anything you'd do is decoration.

## Separate reach from resonance from response

These answer different questions, and collapsing them hides the useful signal:

- **Reach** — did it get distributed?
- **Resonance** — did the people who saw it react, save or share it?
- **Response** — did anyone take a step toward you: follow, click, message, buy?

Content that reaches without resonating is a targeting problem. Content that
resonates without response is usually a missing ask. Content that does all three
is your format — make more of it.

## Compare like with like

Averaging a month of posts across every format produces a number that describes
nothing. Group by format and by the argument being made, then compare within
groups. "Our demo posts outperform our talking heads" is actionable. "Engagement
is up 4%" is not.

## Write the recommendation first

The strongest reports open with three sentences: what we learned, what we're
changing, and what we need a decision on. The charts sit underneath as evidence
for anyone who wants to check the reasoning.

If writing those three sentences is hard, the problem isn't the reporting
template. It's that the month didn't test anything — which is itself the most
useful finding you can surface.

## Keep a record of what was tried

Reporting gets exponentially more valuable when it accumulates. A simple running
log of what was tried, what the hypothesis was and what happened means month six
isn't re-running month two's experiments with new colours.
