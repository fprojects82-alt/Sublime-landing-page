"Repurposing" has come to mean running a long video through a tool that cuts it
into nine vertical clips. That produces nine pieces of content and roughly zero
new ideas, and audiences can tell.

The version that works starts one level up: not with the asset, with the
argument inside it.

## Pull the claims out first

Take the source — a founder interview, a long post, a customer call — and write
down every distinct claim it makes. Most substantial pieces contain somewhere
between four and eight. Each claim is a potential post, and each one deserves
its own treatment rather than a timestamp.

## Match the claim to the format

Claims aren't interchangeable, so the formats shouldn't be either:

- **A claim people will argue with** → a talking-head take, built for comments.
- **A claim that needs proof** → a demo or before/after.
- **A claim that's really a process** → a step-by-step carousel.
- **A claim that's a number or a comparison** → a static graphic that can be
  screenshotted.
- **A claim that's a story** → a creator telling it in their own words.

The same idea, told five ways, reads as a point of view. The same clip, cut five
ways, reads as filler.

## Build the spine, then the variations

Once formats are assigned, order them. Lead with the most arguable claim — it
sets the frame everything else sits inside. Follow with proof. Put the process
pieces in the middle, where people who are now interested will actually read
them. Close the month with the story.

## Leave space for the response

Reserve a slot or two for whatever the audience actually says back. The best
post in a cycle is often a reply to a comment on the second post — and it only
exists if the calendar wasn't already full.

One good idea, properly disassembled, is a comfortable month. The constraint is
rarely production capacity. It's having something to say that survives being
said five different ways.
